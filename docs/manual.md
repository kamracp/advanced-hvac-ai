COOLING LOAD ENGINEERING PHYSICS

Cooling load represents the total heat energy that must be removed from a building to maintain comfort conditions.

Heat enters the building through:

occupants
lighting
equipment
fresh air
walls & glass
infiltration

The HVAC system removes this heat.

TYPES OF HEAT LOAD
1. Sensible Heat

Heat that changes temperature.

Example:

hot air temperature reduction
wall heat gain
lighting heat
2. Latent Heat

Heat associated with moisture.

Example:

humidity removal
human perspiration
fresh air moisture
COOLING LOAD FORMULAS
1. Sensible Heat from People

Q = People × Sensible Heat Factor

2. Lighting Load

Q = Area × Lighting Density

3. Equipment Load

Q = Area × Equipment Density

4. Ventilation Load

Q = 1.2 × Airflow × Delta T

5. TR Conversion

1 TR = 3.517 kW

OUTPUT PARAMETERS
Output	Meaning
Sensible People Load	Human sensible heat
Latent People Load	Human moisture load
Lighting Load	Lighting heat
Equipment Load	Equipment heat
Ventilation Load	Outdoor air load
Total Cooling Load	Total HVAC load
Total TR	Refrigeration capacity

PRESSURE DROP PHYSICS

Pressure drop is the resistance faced by air while moving through HVAC ducts.

The fan must overcome this resistance.

Pressure losses occur due to:

duct friction
elbows
transitions
dampers
filters
coils
BERNOULLI PRINCIPLE

Air velocity and pressure are interrelated.

Higher velocity means:

higher dynamic pressure
higher friction loss
higher energy consumption
VELOCITY PRESSURE

Velocity pressure is the kinetic energy of moving air.

Formula:

VP = 0.6 × V²

This comes from fluid dynamics principles.

FAN STATIC PRESSURE

Fan static pressure must overcome:

friction losses
fitting losses
filter losses
coil losses

Insufficient fan static causes:

low airflow
poor cooling
room imbalance

PSYCHROMETRIC PHYSICS

Psychrometrics is the science of moist air.

HVAC systems condition both:

temperature
moisture

Psychrometrics combines:

thermodynamics
heat transfer
moisture physics
DRY BULB TEMPERATURE

Actual air temperature measured by thermometer.

Represents sensible heat.

RELATIVE HUMIDITY

Represents moisture percentage in air.

High RH causes:

discomfort
sweating
condensation
microbial growth
WET BULB TEMPERATURE

Represents evaporative cooling capability.

Used in:

cooling towers
evaporative cooling
air washers
DEW POINT TEMPERATURE

Temperature at which moisture condenses into water.

If any HVAC surface falls below dew point:

water condensation occurs
duct sweating occurs
insulation becomes critical
ENTHALPY

Represents total heat energy of air.

Includes:

sensible heat
latent heat

Enthalpy is critical for:

coil sizing
energy calculations
fresh air analysis

HVAC ENERGY PHYSICS

HVAC systems consume energy mainly for:

chillers
pumps
fans
cooling towers

Energy analysis helps identify:

operating cost
efficiency improvement
energy saving opportunities
COP (COEFFICIENT OF PERFORMANCE)

COP = Cooling Output / Electrical Input

Higher COP means:

better efficiency
lower electricity consumption
ENERGY RELATIONSHIP

Energy = Power × Time

Therefore:

longer operating hours increase energy
low efficiency increases energy cost
HVAC ENERGY OPTIMIZATION

Energy can be reduced by:

improving COP
reducing airflow resistance
increasing Delta T
optimizing control systems
improving insulation
